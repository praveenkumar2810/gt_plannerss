Why Zaid GT is challenging
Small area extent
Zaid crops are grown in fragmented, small fields (<1 ha average). Many fields below S2 MMU (minimum mapping unit). Use very high res reference where available.
Short phenological window
Moong/Urad mature in 60 days. If you miss the flowering-to-pod window (roughly 30–45 DAP), the field will look bare or harvested. Field visits must be precise within ±5 days.
Spectral confusion with fallow
Low-canopy summer crops and fallow land have overlapping NDVI values (<0.35). Use red-edge bands (B5, B6, B7) and NDRE index for better separation in S2 classification.
S2 advantage in Zaid
Pre-monsoon sky is the clearest of the year — Mar–Apr cloud cover <10% across most of India. You can expect 8–9 clean S2 passes in a 45-day Zaid window, same as Rabi.
Major soil types & crop suitability
Understanding soil type helps design GT stratification — different soils support different crop assemblages and yield levels

Alluvial Soil

Most fertile and widely distributed. Found across the Indo-Gangetic plains. Rich in potash, phosphoric acid; deficient in nitrogen. Old alluvium (Bangar) vs new alluvium (Khadar).

States: UP, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana, West Bengal, Assam, Uttarakhand
Best for: Wheat, Paddy, Sugarcane, Maize, Oilseeds, Pulses

Black Soil (Regur)

Self-ploughing clay soil. High water retention, rich in calcium, magnesium, potash. Poor in nitrogen, phosphorus, and organic matter. Swells when wet, cracks when dry.

States: Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat, AP, Telangana, Karnataka (Deccan trap)
Best for: Cotton, Soybean, Jowar, Linseed, Sunflower, Pulses

Red & Yellow Soil

Formed by weathering of crystalline rocks. Red due to iron oxide diffusion. Porous, friable, deficient in nitrogen, phosphorus, humus. Yellow when hydrated.

States: AP, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh
Best for: Groundnut, Bajra, Ragi, Tobacco, Vegetables

Laterite & Lateritic Soil

Formed in high temperature and heavy rainfall with alternating wet/dry seasons. Leached of silica and bases. Rich in iron and aluminium oxides. Acidic, low fertility.

States: Kerala, Karnataka coast, Tamil Nadu hills, Assam, Odisha, WB hills
Best for: Cashew, Tea, Coffee, Rubber, Tapioca

Desert & Arid Soil

Sandy, poor in organic matter, soluble salts present. Low moisture retention, high permeability. Calcareous subsoil. Phosphate levels normal; nitrogen deficient.

States: Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat, Haryana (western)
Best for: Bajra, Jowar, Moth bean, Groundnut (with irrigation)

Forest & Mountain Soil

Found in hilly terrains under forest cover. Heterogeneous — varies by altitude, parent rock, vegetation. Generally acidic, rich in humus in upper slopes.

States: HP, J&K, Uttarakhand, NE states, Western Ghats
Best for: Tea, Coffee, Spices, Fruits (Apple, Apricot), Maize

Deltaic Alluvial Soil

Deposited by river deltas — very fine-grained, highly fertile. Saline near coasts. Rich in nitrogen, potash. Supports double/triple cropping with irrigation.

States: West Bengal (Ganga delta), Odisha (Mahanadi), AP (Krishna-Godavari), TN (Cauvery)
Best for: Paddy (all seasons), Jute, Sugarcane, Banana

Terai Soil

Found along the foothills of the Himalayas. Moist, heavy soils rich in nitrogen and organic matter. Swampy and poorly drained in places. Recently cleared forest land.

States: Uttarakhand, UP (north), Bihar (north), West Bengal (north), Assam
Best for: Paddy, Jute, Sugarcane, Wheat, Mustard

Mixed Red & Black Soil

Transition zone between red soil and black soil regions. Characteristics vary with parent material. Moderate fertility, generally deficient in phosphorus.

States: Southern Maharashtra, Karnataka, AP (parts)
Best for: Cotton, Jowar, Groundnut, Pulses

Peaty & Marshy Soil

High organic matter (humus), heavy and black. Acidic. Found in areas with high rainfall and poor drainage. Waterlogged conditions for most of the year.

States: Kerala (Kuttanad), Sundarbans (WB), Bihar (north), Odisha coast
Best for: Paddy (flooded), Coconut, Jute
GT sampling implication by soil type
When stratifying GT samples, soil type is a mandatory stratification variable. The same crop grown on black soil vs. red soil produces significantly different spectral signatures due to soil background reflectance bleeding into the canopy signal — especially during early vegetative stages when canopy cover is <60%. Always record soil type during GT collection and ensure at least 10–15 GT samples per soil × crop combination in mixed-soil states like Maharashtra, AP, and Karnataka.

How to use ACZ in GT design
Agro-climatic zones define the crop-environment interaction envelope. For multi-state classifiers, always ensure GT samples span all ACZs present in the study area. A wheat classifier trained only on Trans-Gangetic plains (ACZ 6) data will underperform on Central Plateau (ACZ 8) wheat due to different soil background, canopy architecture, and phenological timing. The recommended minimum is 15–20 GT points per crop per ACZ, not just per state.

GT field collection protocol
Standard operating guidelines for field teams — Sentinel-2 10m bands

Minimum sample size

Per crop per ACZ: ≥30 pure-crop polygons or ≥50 GPS points. For rare crops (millets, specialty pulses) ≥20 is acceptable. Ensure spatial distribution across soil types and irrigation classes.

Polygon vs point

Collect field boundary polygons where possible. For fields <0.5 ha: center-point + area estimate. For fields >1 ha: walk the boundary. Each GT point must represent a pure 3×3 pixel (>30m buffer from field edge for S2 10m bands).

Mandatory attributes

Crop name (local + botanical), variety, sowing date (approx), irrigation type (rainfed / canal / borewell), growth stage at visit, intercrop / mixed crop status, field size, soil type (visible), geotagged photo.

Soil type recording

Record soil type at each GT point — even a visual assessment (black/red/sandy/alluvial). This is critical for multi-state classifiers. Use soil texture as secondary attribute. At minimum cross-check with NBSS&LUP soil map.

ACZ stratification

Ensure GT samples span ALL ACZs present in study area. Do not cluster samples in accessible zones. Use ACZ boundaries as mandatory stratification layer in sampling design. Min 15–20 pts per crop per ACZ.

Satellite pass sync

Plan visits 0–2 days AFTER a confirmed cloud-free S2 pass. Check 7-day forecast before mobilizing. For Kharif, pre-identify 2–3 alternate S2 dates in your 45-day window as monsoon cloud backup.

Mixed crop flag

Flag all mixed/intercrop fields with MIXED tag. Exclude from primary training set unless building a mixed class. Record dominant + secondary crop combination for later use in multi-label classification.

Crop code standard

All teams must use the same crop code list + local name lookup table before departure. Use FASAL or ICAR crop codes. Single enumerator naming creates duplicates that break label encoders in ML pipelines.
